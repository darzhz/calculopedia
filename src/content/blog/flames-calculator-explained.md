FLAMES is the classic name-compatibility game that everyone played in school. Take two names, follow a few simple rules, and one of six fates appears: **F**riends, **L**overs, **A**ffectionate, **M**arriage, **E**nemies or **S**iblings.

## The rules

1. Write both names, ignoring spaces.
2. **Cross out** every letter that appears in both names.
3. **Count** the letters that are left unmatched.
4. Step around the FLAMES circle and **remove the letter at each count**, until one letter remains.

## Worked example: RAM and SITA

    RAM:  R A M
    SITA: S I T A

'A' appears in both names, so it's cancelled. The unmatched letters are R, M, S, I, T → **5**.

Now count around **FLAMES**, removing every 5th letter:

    F L A M E S  →  remove E
    F L A M S    →  remove M
    F L A S      →  remove S
    F L A        →  remove L
    F A          →  remove A

The last letter is **F → Friends**.

## What each letter means

| Letter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| F | Friends |
| L | Lovers |
| A | Affectionate |
| M | Marriage |
| E | Enemies |
| S | Siblings |

The fewer common letters, the bigger the count, and the more passes around the circle — which changes the final letter completely. Try it with full names, first names, or nicknames; the results differ.

## The honest disclaimer

FLAMES is pure entertainment — a number trick, not a relationship forecast. It's a great party game and a fun trip down memory lane, but love is far too complicated for six letters.
