APR is the true yearly cost of a loan. The quoted "rate" is just the interest — APR adds upfront fees so you can compare loans honestly.

## The approximation

    APR ≈ rate + (2 × m × F) ÷ (P × (N + 1)) × 100

- **m** = payments per year
- **F** = total upfront fees
- **P** = loan amount
- **N** = total number of payments

## Worked example

₹1,00,000 loan at 8% with ₹2,000 fees over 5 years (60 payments):

    APR ≈ 8 + (2 × 12 × 2,000) ÷ (1,00,000 × 61) × 100
        ≈ 8 + 0.79 ≈ 8.79%

So although the rate reads "8%", you're really paying about **8.79%** per year.

## Why it matters

Two loans can both quote 8% yet have very different APRs once fees differ. Always compare **APR to APR** — the lower APR loan is cheaper overall.
