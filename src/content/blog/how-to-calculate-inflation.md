Inflation is the gradual rise in prices, and it quietly shrinks what your money buys. The same note buys less every year.

## The formula

    Future value = amount × (1 + rate)^years

## Worked example

₹1,00,000 at an average 6% inflation over 6 years (2020 → 2026):

    Future value = 1,00,000 × (1.06)⁶ ≈ ₹1,41,852

A basket of goods costing ₹1,00,000 in 2020 costs about ₹1,41,852 in 2026. Your money didn't grow — prices did.

## Reading the result

- **Future value** — the nominal amount you'd need later to buy the same things.
- **Price multiplier** — how many times prices grew (here 1.42×).
- **Real loss** — the extra ₹41,852 is purchasing power erosion, not value.

## Why it matters

- **Savings** — an FD paying 6% in a 6% inflation year has a **zero real return**.
- **Salary** — a 5% raise in a 6% inflation year is a real pay *cut*.
- **Planning** — retirement and education costs must be inflation-adjusted.
