A HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit) lets you borrow against your home's equity. You only pay interest on what you draw, and payments are often flexible.

## The formulas

    Monthly interest = balance × (annual rate ÷ 12)

    Amortized payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ)
    r = monthly rate, n = months, P = drawn balance

## Interest-only worked example

$50,000 drawn at 8.5%:

    Monthly interest = 50,000 × 0.085 ÷ 12 ≈ $354/month

The balance stays flat — you're not paying down principal.

## Amortized worked example

Same $50,000 over 10 years:

    r = 0.085 ÷ 12 ≈ 0.00708, n = 120
    Payment ≈ $620/month

## Why HELOC rates can bite

HELOCs are usually **variable rate**, tied to a benchmark like SOFR. The teaser rate can rise sharply. And because the loan is secured by your home, falling behind puts the house at risk — only draw what you can repay.

This is an estimate. Actual draw periods, rate caps and payment structures vary by lender.
