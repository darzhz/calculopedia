Whether you're financing a new car or a used one, the monthly payment is the number that decides whether the deal fits your budget. Here's how it works.

## The formula

    Monthly payment = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

- **P** = financed amount = car price − down payment
- **r** = monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
- **n** = number of months (years × 12)

This is the same EMI formula used for home and personal loans — a fixed payment that covers both principal and interest every month.

## Worked example

Say the car costs **₹8,00,000** and you put down **₹1,50,000** on a **5-year loan at 9.5%**:

- P = 8,00,000 − 1,50,000 = **₹6,50,000**
- r = 9.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = **0.00792**
- n = 5 × 12 = **60 months**
- Payment = 6,50,000 × 0.00792 × (1.00792)⁶⁰ / ((1.00792)⁶⁰ − 1) ≈ **₹13,654/month**

Over 60 months you pay ₹8,19,240, of which **₹1,69,240 is interest**. The car's total cost is price + interest = **₹9,69,240**.

## What changes the payment

- **Down payment** — every rupee you put down is a rupee you don't finance and don't pay interest on. Going from 10% to 20% down can cut your monthly payment noticeably.
- **Loan term** — 48 vs 60 months: the longer term drops the monthly payment but adds a full year of interest charges.
- **Interest rate** — a 1% lower rate on a 5-year loan typically saves several thousand in interest.

## Rule of thumb

A common guideline is to keep your total car budget (payment + fuel + insurance + maintenance) under **15% of your monthly take-home pay**. So first check your monthly income — then back into a car price that keeps the payment affordable.
