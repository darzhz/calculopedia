"Doctors say I'm 30 weeks, but I only conceived 28 weeks ago" — this confuses nearly everyone. Here's how pregnancy weeks actually work.

## Pregnancy is counted from your LMP

Gestational age counts from the **first day of your last menstrual period (LMP)**, not from conception (which happens about two weeks later). That's why you're "2 weeks pregnant" before you've even conceived.

## The formula

    Gestational age = days since LMP ÷ 7

### Worked example

LMP was **15 January 2026**, today is **14 August 2026**:

    211 days ÷ 7 = 30 weeks and 1 day

So you're **30 weeks and 1 day pregnant**, and the pregnancy is about 75% complete (211 ÷ 280).

## Reverse from a due date

Only have a due date? Subtract 280 days:

    LMP = due date − 280 days

So a due date of **22 October 2026** gives an LMP of **15 January 2026** — same result as above.

## Trimesters

| Trimester | Weeks | Highlights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 1–13 | Major organs form; morning sickness common |
| Second | 14–27 | Movement felt; the scan can reveal the sex |
| Third | 28–40 | Rapid growth; preparation for birth |

A full-term pregnancy is **40 weeks**. Only about 4% of babies are born exactly on the due date — most arrive within two weeks either side, so treat the date as an estimate.

## Accuracy

Dating from LMP assumes a regular 28-day cycle. If your cycle is irregular, or you want the most accurate date, a first-trimester ultrasound is the gold standard. Your doctor's dating wins over any formula.
