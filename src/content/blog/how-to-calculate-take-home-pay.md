Your salary offer shows a **gross figure**, but what actually lands in your bank is the **take-home pay** (net salary). Here's how to work it out — and how much tax is really taking.

## The steps

1. Start with your **gross annual salary** (CTC).
2. Subtract the **standard deduction**: ₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old.
3. In the **old regime**, also subtract deductions like 80C, 80D and HRA.
4. Apply the **tax slab rates** to the taxable amount.
5. Add **4% health & education cess**.
6. **Take-home = gross − total tax.**

## Worked example (new regime)

For **₹12,00,000 gross**:

- Taxable = 12,00,000 − 75,000 = **₹11,25,000**
- Slab tax on ₹11,25,000 = **₹62,500**
- But taxable ≤ ₹12,00,000, so the **87A rebate** reduces tax to ₹0
- Total tax = ₹0 → **monthly take-home ≈ ₹1,00,000**

For **₹15,00,000 gross**:

- Taxable = ₹14,25,000 → slab tax **₹93,750**
- Cess 4% = ₹3,750 → **total tax ₹97,500**
- Annual take-home = ₹14,02,500 → **monthly ≈ ₹1,16,875**

## New vs old regime

| | New regime | Old regime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| Slab rates | Lower | Higher |
| Deductions (80C, HRA…) | No | Yes |
| Best for | Salary-only income | High deductions |

For most salaried earners with no big deductions, the **new regime** wins. Run both through the [Take-Home Pay Calculator](/finance/take-home-pay-calculator/) to compare your exact numbers.

## A quicker estimate

- Below ₹12 lakh gross (new regime): tax is usually **₹0** thanks to the rebate.
- Above that, your **effective rate** is well below the top slab rate — it's the *average* across all slabs, not the marginal 30%.
- Rough guide: 12–20 lakh gross → effective rate around 7–11%.
