VO₂max is the best single measure of aerobic fitness — the maximum oxygen your body can use per minute during exercise. You can estimate it without running a single test.

## The resting-heart-rate method

    VO₂max ≈ 15.3 × (max HR ÷ resting HR)
    Max HR = 220 − age

A low resting heart rate means a strong heart that pumps more with each beat — which correlates with high fitness.

## Worked example

A 35-year-old with a 65 bpm resting HR:

    Max HR = 185
    VO₂max = 15.3 × (185 ÷ 65) ≈ 43.5 mL/kg/min

## What the numbers mean (30–39 age bracket)

| Category | Men | Women |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excellent | 52+ | 41+ |
| Good | 44–51 | 35–40 |
| Average | 38–43 | 31–34 |
| Fair | 32–37 | 25–30 |
| Below average | <32 | <25 |

43.5 lands in the *Good* range for a man.

## More accurate methods

Field tests — the Cooper 12-minute run, Rockport one-mile walk, or a treadmill test — are more accurate. Lab gas analysis is the gold standard. Regular aerobic training lowers resting HR and raises your score.
