The break-even point is where revenue exactly equals costs — no profit, no loss. Sell more and you're profitable.

## The formula

    Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit)

- **Fixed costs** — rent, salaries, utilities. Don't change with sales.
- **Variable costs** — materials, packaging, per-unit labour.
- **Contribution margin** = price − variable cost: what each unit contributes toward fixed costs.

## Worked example

A bakery has ₹5,00,000 of fixed costs, sells cakes at ₹250 with ₹150 of variable cost each:

    Contribution = 250 − 150 = ₹100 per cake
    Break-even = 5,00,000 ÷ 100 = 5,000 cakes
    Break-even revenue = 5,000 × 250 = ₹12,50,000

The bakery must sell **5,000 cakes** (₹12,50,000 in sales) before earning its first rupee of profit.

## How to improve break-even

- **Raise prices** — bigger contribution per unit.
- **Cut variable costs** — better suppliers, less waste.
- **Lower fixed costs** — cheaper rent, smaller team.

A lower break-even means less risk: you need fewer sales just to survive.
