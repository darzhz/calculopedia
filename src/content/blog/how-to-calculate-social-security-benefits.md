Social Security pays a monthly retirement benefit based on lifetime earnings. The benefit — the Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) — is computed from your Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME) using a progressive formula.

## Step 1 — AIME

Take your **35 highest-earning years**, adjust each year's wages for average wage growth, and divide by **420 months**. Zeroes fill in if you worked fewer than 35 years.

## Step 2 — the bend-point formula (2025)

    PIA = 90% × first part + 32% × second part + 15% × the rest

    First bend point:   $1,226
    Second bend point:  $7,392

## Worked example

An AIME of $5,000:

    90% × 1,226             = $1,103.40
    32% × (5,000 − 1,226)   = $1,207.68
    PIA                     = $2,311.08/month

## Why it's progressive

Low earners get a **90% replacement** on their first dollars; high earners only **15%** on earnings above the second bend point. Social Security deliberately replaces a larger share of income for lower earners.

## Adjusting your benefit

- **Early** (age 62): up to ~30% less than PIA.
- **Full retirement age** (67 for most): exactly PIA.
- **Delayed** (to 70): ~8% more per year past full retirement age.

This is an estimate using standard bend points. Use your actual earnings record at ssa.gov for a precise number. Estimate yours with the [Social Security Benefits Calculator](/finance/social-security-calculator/).
