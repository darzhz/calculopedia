Max heart rate is the ceiling your heart can reach under all-out effort. It anchors every heart-rate training zone, and it's easy to estimate.

## The formulas

    Classic:   Max HR = 220 − age
    Tanaka:    Max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age

The classic is what most people know. Tanaka matches real measured values better for most adults.

| Age | 220 − age | Tanaka |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 200 | 194 |
| 30 | 190 | 187 |
| 40 | 180 | 180 |
| 50 | 170 | 173 |

## Worked example

A 30-year-old:

    Max HR = 220 − 30 = 190 bpm

## Training zones from max HR

| Zone | % of max | Example at 190 bpm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Recovery | 50–60% | 95–114 |
| 2 — Fat burn | 60–70% | 114–133 |
| 3 — Cardio | 70–80% | 133–152 |
| 4 — High intensity | 80–90% | 152–171 |
| 5 — Max effort | 90–100% | 171–190 |

A heart-rate strap or watch measures your real max far better than any formula. If you're new to intense exercise or have heart conditions, check with a doctor first.
