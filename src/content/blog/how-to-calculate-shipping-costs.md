Shipping prices look mysterious, but they're built from a small set of components. Once you know them, you can estimate almost any shipment.

## The formula

    Shipping cost ≈ base rate + weight charge + zone fee + package surcharge

### 1. Base rate

Every shipment carries a minimum handling charge. It's what makes a tiny envelope still cost a few dollars.

### 2. Weight charge

The per-kilogram (or per-pound) rate × your package weight. Two wrinkles:

- **Dimensional weight** — carriers charge by volume for large, light boxes: `length × width × height ÷ divisor`. If that's bigger than real weight, you pay for it.
- **Tiered rates** — the per-kg price usually drops as packages get heavier.

### 3. Zone fee

Distance matters. Same-city is cheapest, and international is the most expensive band. Carriers publish zone tables for every origin/destination pair.

### 4. Package surcharge

Envelopes and standard boxes are cheap. **Oversized** items, unusual shapes or non-standard packaging add fees.

## Worked example

A **2 kg standard box, UPS, regional delivery**:

    Base rate       $9.00
    Weight charge   $1.20/kg × 2 kg = $2.40
    Zone fee        $1.50
    Package charge  $0.50
    Estimated total ≈ $13.40

## Why actual quotes differ

Estimates are planning figures. The real price depends on your **exact postal codes**, **dimensional weight**, **service speed** (ground vs overnight) and any **business discounts**. Always confirm with the carrier's quote tool.

## How to save

- **Densify your packaging** — fewer, smaller boxes mean lower dimensional weight.
- **Go ground** — express rates are multiples of ground for no better reliability on most routes.
- **Use carrier packaging** — own-brand boxes avoid some surcharges.
- **Compare routes** — USPS often wins for small light parcels; UPS/FedEx for heavier business shipping.
