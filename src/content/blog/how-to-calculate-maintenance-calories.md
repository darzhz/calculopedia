Your **maintenance calories** are the exact number of calories you can eat every day without gaining or losing weight. Once you know this number, losing or gaining weight becomes a simple adjustment on top of it.

## The formula

    Maintenance calories = BMR × activity factor

### Step 1 — BMR

**Mifflin-St Jeor** is the most accurate common formula:

    Men:   10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
    Women: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

### Step 2 — activity factor

| Activity | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Sedentary | 1.2 |
| Lightly active | 1.375 |
| Moderately active | 1.55 |
| Very active | 1.725 |
| Extra active | 1.9 |

## Worked example

A **30-year-old man, 175 cm, 75 kg, moderately active**:

    BMR = 10×75 + 6.25×175 − 5×30 + 5 = 750 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 ≈ 1,699 kcal
    Maintenance = 1,699 × 1.55 ≈ 2,633 kcal/day

So he can eat ~2,633 kcal daily and stay at 75 kg.

## Cut and bulk

- **Lose fat:** eat ~15% below maintenance ≈ 2,238 kcal. A 500 kcal daily deficit ≈ 0.5 kg/week.
- **Gain muscle:** eat ~15% above maintenance ≈ 3,028 kcal, combined with strength training.

## Accuracy tips

- Estimates drift by up to ±200 kcal between people. Track your weight for **two weeks** at your predicted maintenance and adjust by 100–200 kcal if the scale moves.
- Weigh yourself daily and take a weekly average — day-to-day water weight hides the trend.
- Recalculate whenever your weight changes by more than ~5 kg.
