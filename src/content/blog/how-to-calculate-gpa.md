Your **GPA** is the single number that summarizes your grades — and it's a weighted average, meaning high-credit courses pull the number more than low-credit ones.

## The formula

    GPA = Σ (grade points × credit hours) ÷ Σ credit hours

Multiply each course's grade points by its credits, add everything up, then divide by the total credits.

## Grade points (US 4.0 scale)

| Grade | Points | Grade | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A+ / A / O | 4.0 | C+ | 2.3 |
| A- | 3.7 | C | 2.0 |
| B+ | 3.3 | C- | 1.7 |
| B | 3.0 | D | 1.0 |
| B- | 2.7 | F | 0 |

## Worked example

Four courses, all **3 credits**: A, B+, A-, B.

    Points = (4.0×3) + (3.3×3) + (3.7×3) + (3.0×3) = 42.0
    Credits = 12
    GPA = 42.0 ÷ 12 = 3.5

## Indian 10-point scale (CGPA)

Indian universities use a 10-point scale: **O = 10, A+ = 9, A = 8, B+ = 7**, and so on. The calculation is the same, just with different point values. A common percentage conversion is **CGPA × 9.5**, though rules vary by university.

## GPA vs CGPA

- **GPA** — your average for one term or semester.
- **CGPA** — your **cumulative** average across all terms.

Both use the same weighted-average formula; CGPA is just the running total.

## Tips

- **High-credit courses move your GPA most** — prioritize them.
- **Exclude non-credit courses** by setting credits to 0 (audit classes, electives).
- **Retaking a failed course** usually replaces or averages the grade and can lift your GPA significantly.
