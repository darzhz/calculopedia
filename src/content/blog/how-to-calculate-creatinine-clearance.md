**Creatinine clearance (CrCl)** estimates how well your kidneys filter creatinine, a waste product from muscle metabolism. It's a key gauge of kidney function and guides drug dosing.

## The Cockcroft-Gault formula

    Men:   CrCl = (140 − age) × weight(kg) ÷ (72 × serum creatinine mg/dL)
    Women: the same result × 0.85

## Worked example

A **40-year-old man, 70 kg**, serum creatinine **1.0 mg/dL**:

    CrCl = (140 − 40) × 70 ÷ (72 × 1.0)
         = 7,000 ÷ 72 ≈ 97.2 mL/min

A **40-year-old woman** with the same numbers:

    97.2 × 0.85 ≈ 82.6 mL/min

## Reading the result (CKD stages)

| CrCl (mL/min) | Kidney function |
| --- | --- |
| ≥ 90 | Normal |
| 60–89 | Mildly reduced |
| 30–59 | Moderately reduced |
| 15–29 | Severely reduced |
| < 15 | Kidney failure |

The 0.85 factor for women reflects lower typical muscle mass and creatinine production.

## Limitations

- Cockcroft-Gault **estimates** clearance; it's not a measured test.
- Less accurate for the very elderly, obese, or patients with fluid overload (edema).
- Modern practice often prefers the **CKD-EPI** equation for estimated GFR.
- Serum creatinine fluctuates with hydration, diet and muscle mass — one reading should never be judged in isolation.

> This information is for general education and is not medical advice. Always discuss kidney-function results with your doctor.
