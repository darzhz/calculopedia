GDP has two versions — nominal and real — and the difference is price changes.

## Nominal vs real

- **Nominal GDP** — output at current prices. Rises with output *or* prices.
- **Real GDP** — output at base-year prices. Strips out inflation to show *actual production* growth.

## The formula

    Real GDP = Nominal GDP ÷ GDP deflator × 100

The **GDP deflator** is a price index set to 100 in the base year. A deflator of 120 means prices have risen 20% since then.

## Worked example

A country's nominal GDP is ₹240 lakh crore with a deflator of 120:

    Real GDP = 240 ÷ 120 × 100 = ₹200 lakh crore

The ₹40 lakh crore difference is pure price growth — no extra goods were produced.

## Why economists use real GDP

The growth rates in the news are real growth. If nominal GDP grew 10% but prices grew 6%, real growth is only about 4%. Real GDP is what reveals whether an economy is genuinely producing more.
