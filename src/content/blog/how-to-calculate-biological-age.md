Your biological age is how old your body behaves — at the level of heart, lungs and cells — versus your calendar age. Lifestyle habits shift it up or down.

## The scoring method

Start with your calendar age, then add or subtract years for each habit:

| Factor | Better habits | Worse habits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diet | Whole foods: −1 | Processed diet: +2 |
| Exercise | 4+ days/week: −2 | Rarely: +2 |
| Sleep | 7–9 hrs: −1 | Under 6 hrs: +2 |
| Smoking | No: 0 | Yes: +4 |
| Alcohol | Rarely: −1 | Most days: +1 |
| Stress | Rarely: −1 | Chronic: +2 |

## Worked example

A 40-year-old with a good diet, regular exercise, 7–9 hours of sleep, no smoking, occasional alcohol and low stress:

    −1 + −2 + −1 + 0 + 0 + −1 = −5
    Biological age = 40 − 5 = 35

## Real tests vs this estimate

Lab tests measure biological markers — telomere length, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, grip strength. This questionnaire is a useful screen that flags the habits most strongly linked to premature ageing. The biggest wins: quit smoking, add regular exercise, sleep 7–9 hours, and eat mostly whole foods.
