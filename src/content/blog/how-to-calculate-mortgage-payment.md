The payment on a mortgage is more than just the loan. Lenders quote **PITI** — principal, interest, **taxes and insurance** — because those escrow items are collected with your monthly payment. Here's the full calculation.

## Step 1 — Principal & interest

    Monthly P&I = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

- **P** = home price − down payment
- **r** = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
- **n** = number of months (years × 12)

## Step 2 — Add taxes and insurance

- **Property tax:** annual tax ÷ 12
- **Home insurance:** annual premium ÷ 12

## Step 3 — Add PMI (if applicable)

If your down payment is **under 20%**, lenders charge **private mortgage insurance** — about 0.5–1.5% of the loan per year, divided by 12. It drops off once you reach 20% equity.

## Worked example

A **$350,000 home with $70,000 down (20%) at 6.5% for 30 years**:

    P = $280,000,  r = 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.00542,  n = 360
    P&I = $280,000 × 0.00542 × (1.00542)³⁶⁰ / ((1.00542)³⁶⁰ − 1) ≈ $1,770

    Property tax  $3,000/yr  = $250/month
    Home insurance $1,200/yr = $100/month
    20% down → no PMI

    Total monthly payment ≈ $2,120

## The 28% rule

Lenders generally cap your total housing payment at about **28% of gross monthly income**. So for the $2,120 payment above, you'd want roughly $7,570/month gross ($90,000+/year).

## Why it matters

The monthly payment is only half the story — the **total interest** on a 30-year mortgage often exceeds the home price itself. Try a 15-year term or a larger down payment: the payment rises, but the interest saved is enormous.
