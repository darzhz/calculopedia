The old rule — "one dog year equals seven human years" — is wrong. Dogs mature far faster in their first two years, then the rate depends on size.

## The modern formula

    Year 1  → 15 human years
    Year 2  → +9 (so 24 at age 2)
    Year 3+ → +4 to +7 per year by breed size

| Breed size | Years added after age 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Small (<10 kg) | +4 |
| Medium (10–25 kg) | +5 |
| Large (25–40 kg) | +6 |
| Giant (>40 kg) | +7 |

## Worked example

A **5-year-old medium** dog:

    24 + (5 − 2) × 5 = 24 + 15 = 39 human years

A 5-year-old giant breed: 24 + 3 × 7 = **45**. Big dogs age faster.

## Why big dogs age faster

Larger breeds hit old age sooner — Great Danes are senior around 7, while a Chihuahua is still sprightly at 10. Their faster growth and higher metabolic stress take a toll on organs, joints and lifespan.

## DNA-level math

Scientists have even matched dogs to humans at the DNA level with epigenetic clocks. The pattern matches the formula above: rapid early aging that flattens — and confirms small breeds genuinely live longer.
