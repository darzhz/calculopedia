Cost of sales — also called cost of goods sold (COGS) — is the direct cost of the products you sold in a period. Subtract it from revenue and you get gross profit.

## The formula

    COGS = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory

Everything you could have sold this period is what you started with, plus what you bought, minus what's still left.

## Worked example

A store opens the year with ₹2,00,000 of stock, buys ₹8,00,000 more, and ends with ₹1,50,000:

    Available for sale = 2,00,000 + 8,00,000 = ₹10,00,000
    COGS = 10,00,000 − 1,50,000 = ₹8,50,000

If revenue was ₹12,00,000, gross profit = **₹3,50,000**.

## Why it matters

- **Gross margin** = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue — how efficiently you buy and sell.
- **Tax** — COGS is a deductible business expense.
- **Pricing** — you can't price profitably without knowing true unit cost.

For service businesses, cost of sales is usually the wages and materials directly tied to delivering the service — not rent or admin.
