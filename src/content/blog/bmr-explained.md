**Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** is the energy your body burns at complete rest — the calories keeping your heart beating, lungs working and cells repairing. It's usually **60–75% of your total daily burn**, before you move a muscle.

## The Mifflin-St Jeor formula

    Men:   10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
    Women: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

A **30-year-old man, 175 cm, 75 kg**:

    BMR = 750 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 ≈ 1,699 kcal

## The Katch-McArdle formula

    BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg)

Because muscle burns far more energy at rest than fat, this formula — which needs your **body fat percentage** — is often more accurate for athletes and fit people.

## From BMR to daily calories

BMR is your baseline; multiply by your activity level to get **TDEE** (the calories to maintain your weight):

| Activity | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Sedentary | 1.2 |
| Lightly active | 1.375 |
| Moderately active | 1.55 |
| Very active | 1.725 |
| Extra active | 1.9 |

A BMR of 1,699 with moderate activity → **~2,633 kcal/day** to stay the same weight.

## Common BMR mistakes

- **Treating BMR as your calorie target** — it's not. BMR is the floor; your real needs add activity.
- **Not recalculating** — after losing or gaining ~5 kg, your BMR changes.
- **Ignoring muscle** — strength training raises BMR over time, which is why muscle-builders can eat more.
