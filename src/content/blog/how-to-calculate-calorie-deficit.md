A calorie deficit is simply eating fewer calories than you burn. That shortfall forces your body to use stored fat for energy — which is what weight loss is.

## The formula

    Target intake = TDEE − deficit
    Weekly loss = (deficit × 7) ÷ 7,700 kg

- **TDEE** — the calories you burn in a day (BMR × activity).
- **Deficit** — how many calories below TDEE you eat.
- **7,700** — the calories in roughly 1 kg of body fat.

## Worked example

A 30-year-old man, 175 cm, 75 kg, moderately active:

    BMR = 10 × 75 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 30 + 5 ≈ 1,699 kcal
    TDEE = 1,699 × 1.55 ≈ 2,633 kcal

With a **500 kcal deficit**:

    Target intake = 2,633 − 500 = 2,133 kcal
    Weekly loss = (500 × 7) ÷ 7,700 ≈ 0.45 kg/week
    5 kg in about 11 weeks

## Choosing a deficit

| Deficit | Weekly loss | Pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 250 kcal | ~0.23 kg | Gentle, easy to sustain |
| 500 kcal | ~0.45 kg | Standard, recommended |
| 1,000 kcal | ~0.9 kg | Fast, harder to stick to |

Most people should stay in the **250–500 kcal** range. Larger deficits risk muscle loss and are hard to sustain. For safety, intake should not drop below **1,200 kcal** per day.
