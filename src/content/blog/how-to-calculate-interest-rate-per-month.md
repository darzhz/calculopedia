An "annual" interest rate is rarely applied once a year — it's charged monthly on loans and credited monthly on deposits. Converting is one division.

## The formula

    Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12
    Monthly interest = principal × monthly rate ÷ 100

## Worked example

Loan of **₹50,000 at 12% per annum**:

    Monthly rate = 12 ÷ 12 = 1%
    Monthly interest = 50,000 × 1% = ₹500
    12 months → ₹6,000 total interest

## Going the other way

Monthly rate to annual? Multiply by 12. A **1% monthly rate** is **12% nominal** per year — but because of monthly compounding, the *effective* annual rate is:

    (1 + 0.01)¹² − 1 ≈ 12.68%

So a "1% monthly" credit card or loan actually costs ~12.68% over a year, not 12%.

## Simple vs compound

- **Simple interest** — interest on the original principal only (what this article uses). Good for quick estimates.
- **Loan amortization** — you pay interest on the shrinking balance, so the interest portion falls every month. Use the [Loan EMI Calculator](/finance/loan-emi-calculator/) for exact repayment.
- **Compounding deposits** — interest is added to the principal each period, so returns accelerate. Use the [FD Calculator](/finance/fd-calculator/) or [Compound Interest Calculator](/finance/compound-interest-calculator/).

## Rule of thumb

For most monthly budgeting and loan comparisons, `annual ÷ 12` is accurate enough — the compounding difference matters only on large balances and long terms.
