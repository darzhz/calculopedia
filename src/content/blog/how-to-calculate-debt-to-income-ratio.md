Your **debt-to-income (DTI) ratio** is the number lenders check first. It measures how much of your income is already promised to debt payments — and therefore how much room you have for a new loan.

## The formula

    DTI = total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income × 100

## Worked example

Gross annual income **₹12,00,000** (₹1,00,000/month), **₹30,000/month** in debt payments:

    DTI = 30,000 ÷ 1,00,000 × 100 = 30%

## What counts as debt

**Include:** loan EMIs, credit-card minimum payments, car loans, rent, alimony.

**Exclude:** utilities, groceries, insurance, phone bills, discretionary spending.

## Lender thresholds

| DTI | Outlook |
| --- | --- |
| Below 36% | Good — approvals are routine |
| 36–43% | Fair — the typical mortgage ceiling |
| Above 43% | High — most lenders will say no |

A common rule: your total EMIs should stay within **~36%** of monthly income, and housing alone within ~28%.

## How to improve your DTI

1. **Clear small, high-interest debts first** — every EMI eliminated lowers the ratio.
2. **Boost income** — a raise or side income directly shrinks the percentage.
3. **Don't open new credit** before a home-loan application — even a small EMI moves the number.

## Why it matters for your EMI

Banks back-calculate your **maximum loan amount** from your DTI. At 36% with ₹1,00,000 monthly income, you're capped at ₹36,000 in total EMIs — which determines how big a loan you can take at a given rate and tenure.
