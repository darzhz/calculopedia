Body Mass Index is the most widely used weight-health metric — and the most misunderstood.

## The formula

```
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
```

For a **70 kg, 175 cm** person: 70 ÷ 1.75² = **22.9**.

## The categories (WHO, adults)

| BMI          | Category      |
| ------------ | ------------- |
| Below 18.5   | Underweight   |
| 18.5 – 24.9  | Normal weight |
| 25 – 29.9    | Overweight    |
| 30 and above | Obese         |

A BMI of 22.9 lands comfortably in "normal". For a 175 cm person, that range corresponds to roughly 56.7–76.3 kg.

## What BMI is good at

It's a fast, cheap, widely comparable **screening** tool. Population studies show strong links between high BMI and conditions like type-2 diabetes, heart disease and joint problems — which is why insurers and health systems rely on it.

## What BMI misses

- **Muscle vs fat** — BMI can't tell them apart. A muscular athlete can be "overweight" with very low body fat.
- **Where the fat is** — belly fat is riskier than fat elsewhere, but BMI ignores distribution. Waist circumference is a better predictor of metabolic risk.
- **Age and ethnicity** — muscle mass declines with age, and healthy BMI ranges vary by ethnicity (many Asian populations have elevated risk at lower BMIs).

## How to use it sensibly

Treat BMI as one data point, not a verdict. Combine it with waist circumference and, ideally, a body-fat measurement. For children and teens, use age- and sex-specific growth charts — the adult categories don't apply.
